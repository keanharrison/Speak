# Speak — User interviews (YC / Mom Test style)

**Who:** Dog owners who care about their dog's health (target: busy owners who delay care when the dog "seems fine").  
**Goal:** Learn how they already notice problems, what they do, what they pay, and what fails — **without pitching Speak**.  
**Length:** 20–30 minutes. You talk ~10%. They talk ~90%.

---

## Rules (do not break)

1. **Talk about their life, not your idea.** Do not describe Speak, kits, dashboards, or AI until the very end — and only if you must.
2. **Ask about the past, not the future.** Prefer "Tell me about the last time…" over "Would you…?"
3. **Ignore compliments and hypotheticals.** "That sounds cool" / "I'd definitely use that" is not signal. Redirect to what they *did*.
4. **Hunt for commitment.** Money spent, time spent, tools used, people called — not opinions.
5. **Dig for specifics.** Dates, sequence, dollars, who else was involved.

**Banned question shapes:** "Would you use…?", "Do you think…?", "How much would you pay for…?", "What features do you want?"

**Recovery line when they go vague or complimentary:**  
"Got it — walk me through the last time that actually happened."

---

## Opening (30 seconds)

> "I'm researching how dog owners handle health stuff between vet visits. Not selling anything. Curious about what you've actually done recently. OK if I ask about a few specific times?"

---

## The questions (18)

### Their dog + recent reality

1. Tell me about your dog(s) — age, breed, anything health-related you've dealt with in the last year.
2. Walk me through the **last time** you worried something might be wrong with them. What did you notice first?
3. What did you do next — step by step? (Who did you call/text? Google? Wait? Vet?)
4. How long did you wait between first worry and actually doing something? What made you wait or act?

### How hard / how often / why it matters

5. What's the hardest part about figuring out whether your dog needs the vet?
6. How often has something like that last episode come up in the past year?
7. What was at stake that time — money, guilt, time off work, fear you waited too long?

### Current workarounds (what they do *today*)

8. How do you keep track of your dog's health day to day, if at all? (Show me if you can — notes app, photos, calendar, nothing.)
9. Tell me about the **last time** you looked up a dog symptom or lab result online. What did you search, and what did you do with what you found?
10. What have you already tried for peace of mind between visits (insurance, wellness plans, at-home tests, wearables, Facebook groups, etc.)? What did each cost, and what happened?
11. Walk me through how you usually communicate with your vet's office — last real example (phone, portal, email, text). What worked or broke?

### Money and effort already spent

12. What's the most you've spent in the last 2 years on something that was basically "I just wanted to know they were OK" (not emergency surgery)?
13. Tell me about a time you paid for a vet visit (or test) that felt unnecessary in hindsight — or a time you *didn't* go and later regretted it.

### Insurance / coverage (behavior only)

14. Do you have pet insurance? If yes: tell me about the **last time** you filed or thought about filing a claim. If no: what made you skip it?

### Commitment signals (still no pitch)

15. In the last 6 months, what have you personally paid for or spent serious time on to reduce worry about your dog's health?
16. Who else in your household gets involved when something's wrong — and what did they do last time?

### Optional close (only if the conversation earned it — still Mom Test)

17. If this problem got 10× worse next month, what would you actually do first?
18. Is there anyone else I should talk to who's dealt with something like what you described?

---

## If they ask "What are you building?"

Deflect early:

> "Still figuring that out — trying to understand the problem first. Can I ask one more about [their last episode]?"

Only after questions 1–16, if needed: one sentence, then stop and ask what *they've done* that relates — don't demo.

---

## What counts as signal (write these down)

| Signal | Example |
|---|---|
| Strong | Spent $X, drove to emergency, set calendar reminders, bought a kit, called vet 3× |
| Medium | Googled for an hour, asked a friend, monitored for days |
| Weak | "I'd probably…", "That sounds useful", "I wish someone would…" |
| Noise | Compliments, feature wishlists, "my friend would love this" |

---

## After each interview (5 minutes)

Capture only:

1. Last concrete scare + what they did
2. Current workaround + cost/time
3. Biggest friction in their own words (quote)
4. Any real money/time commitment in last 6–12 months
5. One thing that surprised you

Do **not** score "interest in Speak." You didn't pitch.
